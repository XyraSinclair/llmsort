Structured Judgement

How machine opinion becomes measurement — instruments, invariances, solvers, and the economics of asking.

2026-08-07 snapshot every number cites committed evidence board live at pairwiseratio.org

§1The problem

Most decisions reduce to ordering a shortlist: which candidate, which design, which risk, which idea. The information that matters is rarely the order alone — it is how much better, and how sure. LLMs made opinions nearly free. They did not make them trustworthy: an elicited opinion moves with presentation order, with wording, with who seems to be asking. Cheap judgement at scale is only valuable if you can tell judgement from echo.

Every obvious elicitation breaks somewhere specific:

Naive methods and their measured failure
ApproachWhat breaks
“Rate each item 1–10”Miscalibrated and anchor-dependent; scores cluster at 7–8; no error bars
“Sort this list” in one promptPosition bias, context limits, silently dropped or hallucinated items
“Which is better, A or B?” over pairsOrdinal only — throws away magnitude; naive schedules cost O(n²)
Elo / Bradley–Terry over winsBetter aggregation, still magnitude-blind, and passive about which pair to ask next

The claim of this document: judgement is an engineering discipline with the same shape as experimental physics — instruments, invariances, error budgets, and a cost per bit. What follows is that space, walked end to end.

§2Judgement as measurement

The reframe everything else follows from: an elicited answer is a noisy reading from an instrument. An instrument is a point in a three-axis space — arity (judge 1, 2, k, or n items per call) × scale (Stevens: nominal, ordinal, interval, ratio) × output form (a point answer, repeated samples, or the full answer-token PMF). Once instruments are typed, they become comparable — and some cells are strictly better per dollar.

The instrument grid, with honest occupancy
CellNote
pairwise · ratio · point✓the founding instrument: “how many times more X does A have than B?”
pairwise · ratio · PMF✓one letter from a 52-token alphabet; the single completion position’s logprobs are the model’s whole judgement distribution
pairwise · ordinal · point / PMF✓direction only — cheapest; strictly less informative, kept as baseline
pointwise rubric scoring◐a control, not a path — anchor drift is the documented disease
k-wise best–worst (MaxDiff)✗pick best AND worst of k → 2k−3 implied pairs per call; the highest-value missing cell
listwise anything✗rejected by design: context limits, position bias, silent drops

The output-form axis is where the free lunch was hiding. A point answer yields at most one bit of order information; a PMF answer returns the model’s whole conditional prior — up to log₂ 52 ≈ 5.7 bits — at the same price. Consuming the distribution instead of one sample from it is measurably sharper:

§3What deserves the name “belief”

A judgement earns the name belief only if it is a fixed point of the transformations that should not matter.

Swap the presentation order. Negate the attribute (“clarity” → “lack of clarity”). Invert the wording (“times more” → “times less” → “what fraction”). None of these changes the question — so a coherent judge’s answer must transform in the known, trivial way. Every deviation is not noise; it is a named bias, and it is measurable.

The clean formalization: ask one question under the full group G = Z₂³ (order × polarity × wording), pull each answer back through the generator’s known equivariance, and decompose by the characters of the group. The orbit mean is the unique G-invariant projection — that is the belief. Every other Fourier coefficient is an orthogonal, named bias, and Parseval makes the accounting exact: mean-square judgement = belief² + Σ bias².

measured · live orbit, one contested pair: gpt-5.4-mini’s largest bias is the order·polarity interaction, −0.552 nats = 22.3% of its judgement energy — invisible to every one-axis probe; claude-sonnet-4.6 on the same pair: 98.7% G-invariant · artifacts/live/orbit-2026-07-05

The group extends beyond the cube: framing spin (does the belief survive a requester who leans?), paraphrase, nuisance edits (a prestige suffix moved one frontier model 0.878 nats), null content, temperature, time. Each is a row in an invariance table; each row is either instrumented with evidence or honestly marked open. The transformation group is the roadmap.

§4From readings to scores

Why ratio questions: log-ratios compose additively. “A is 3× B” and “B is 2× C” imply “A is 6× C” — so every answer becomes a noisy log-space measurement on an edge of a comparison graph, and the full graph over-determines the latent scores. That redundancy is the error-correction budget.

ratio ladder  [1.0 1.05 1.1 1.2 1.3 1.5 1.75 2.1 2.5 3.1 3.9 5.1 6.8 9.2 12.7 18.0 26.0]  — geometric in log space, dense near parity

The solve is robust least squares (IRLS with Huber loss): outlier judgements are down-weighted rather than believed or discarded, and the posterior yields a per-item uncertainty, not just a rank. Where the PMF instrument runs, each observation enters at its measured variance — the model’s own expressed uncertainty — instead of a stated confidence.

Inconsistency itself is decomposed, not averaged away. The Hodge split separates the judgement flow into a gradient part (explainable by scores), a local curl (triangle-auditable cycles: A>B>C>A), and a harmonic part invisible to every triad audit. Each run then reports an experimentalist’s error budget: stat ± posterior · syst order (nats/pair) · syst cyclic (% of energy) — statistical and systematic uncertainty side by side, never silently pooled. A belief whose systematics dominate is not improved by more sampling; it names the transformation to fix.

§5The economics of asking

Information theory sets the floor: a full order of n items needs Θ(n log n) binary comparisons; top-k needs Θ(n). So exhaustive O(n²) schedules are waste, and passive random schedules leave information on the table. The planner treats the comparison graph like a circuit — the next question goes where effective resistance says an answer buys the most certainty about the boundary that matters (top-k, not the whole order). Default budget: 4·n calls.

The planner’s advantage is measured, not assumed — and the measurement is kept honest in both directions:

measured · regret benchmark, two-sided pins with dated history: the planner initially lost to uniform random selection (hub-and-spoke geometry — fixed); it now wins at scarce budgets, ties at medium, and slightly trails random at large budgets on global order · tests/planner_regret.rs

Every run is denominated end to end: comparisons, tokens, nanodollars, stop reason, per-judgement trace. Worst-case price is computable before the run (--estimate, no network). And the marginal price of a judgement can be zero:

§6Judging the judges

If beliefs are fixed points, then judge quality is measurable without ground-truth labels: run the invariance battery and score internal coherence — times a signal axis, so a judge cannot ace the test by refusing to discriminate. That is the Judge Coherence Benchmark: order swap, reciprocal antisymmetry, cyclic frustration, framing spin, polarity, paraphrase, null calibration, nuisance perturbation — 194 calls per model, every rate with its denominator and 95% CI. Because there are no right answers to memorize, labs can hill-climb it without gaming it.

The battery also produced the sharpest single finding in the repo — sycophancy decomposes like magnetism. Susceptibility χ measures how far a judgement moves per unit of applied framing pressure:

measured · gpt-5.4-mini on a clear pair: χ = −0.18 nats (mild reactance, belief survives). Same model, contested pair: zero-field belief exactly 0.000 and χ = +0.64 — a paramagnetic judgement, moving with whoever asks. gemini-2.5-flash holds a direction on that same pair at +0.12 · artifacts/live/spin-probe-2026-07-05

The right question is not “is this model sycophantic?” but “does this judgement have a spontaneous direction, and how much field moves it?” — per judgement, per model, in nats.

§8Where this goes

Attributes are entities too. Compare attributes pairwise under “importance for goal G” and the solver’s latents are a ratio-scale priority vector — the Analytic Hierarchy Process, recovered as a special case, automated end to end from a goal sentence (cardinal weigh, network form anp). The inverse problem also closes: given a ranking you already believe, measure which candidate attributes reconstruct it (explain), and discover the small attribute basis that spans a space (canonize — attributes ranked by transmissibility, whether different judge models recover the same latent from the same words).

Judgement as a communication medium. Raw pairwise evidence is content-addressed into judgment packets that fuse byte-identically — merging the same evidence twice is a no-op — so evidence can be shipped, merged across parties, and re-solved without double-counting. The end state is an interpretable lane: a bounded candidate set, ordered for a declared purpose by named attributes, explicit weights, per-attribute posteriors, and the evidence needed to challenge the result. Today, honestly: a batch judgment compiler for shortlists, with the packet atom and fusion core shipped and the recipient-side contracts still open.
